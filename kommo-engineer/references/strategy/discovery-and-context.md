# Discovery and commercial context

Use this module when the request depends on how the business sells, who it serves, how work moves, or what the current system actually contains.

## Build a small, evidence-linked context view

Capture only the details needed for the decision:

- business unit, offer, customer profile, geography, and sales motion;
- the user's desired outcome and who will own it;
- lifecycle and pipeline stages, entry/exit rules, handoffs, and current next actions;
- source systems, data owners, relevant fields, connected tools, and access limits;
- channels, opt-out/suppression handling, human takeover, and existing automations;
- current baselines and how they were calculated, if a change needs a success measure.

For every conclusion, distinguish **observed**, **user-stated**, **inferred**, and **unknown**. Include a source or record locator and observation date for facts drawn from mutable systems. Ask for missing context only when it blocks a consequential decision; otherwise state a bounded assumption and show how it affects the proposal.

## Discovery sequence

1. Restate the outcome in operational terms: who should do what differently, for which population, and by when.
2. Map the current path from acquisition or request through qualification, handoff, opportunity, outcome, and relevant post-sale steps. Omit irrelevant stages.
3. Check the actual account and process evidence when access exists. Do not assume labels or counts reveal record relationships, field meaning, automation behavior, or message delivery.
4. Identify the earliest evidenced bottleneck, data-quality gap, or control failure. Separate symptoms from causes and cite contrary evidence.
5. List unknowns and decision dependencies, including unavailable permissions, data, integrations, or owner decisions.

## Context-pack boundary

A context pack is a concise working summary, not a new source of truth. Keep source links, dates, owners, and uncertainty next to claims. Do not silently convert one department's convention, an old export, or an attached example into company-wide policy. Request owner confirmation for consequential contradictions.

## Useful output

| Question | Record |
| --- | --- |
| What outcome is requested? | Desired user/business result and target population |
| What is known? | Evidence, source, observation date, confidence |
| What is not known? | Missing facts, access, and owner decisions |
| What is the main constraint? | Evidence-backed bottleneck or competing explanations |
| What is the smallest next decision? | Specific question, read-only validation, or proposed plan |


## Conversation evidence

When the user supplies transcripts, use [conversation-led onboarding](conversation-led-onboarding.md) to separate participant roles, observed process, exceptions and proposals. Preserve sample coverage and counterexamples. Do not require transcripts for every project or turn recurring wording into a universal qualification rule.
